Hello friends, and a heartfelt welcome to the podcast 'Mythic Tales and Today's Truth.' I am Kavya, your storyteller, and I will walk beside you through this entire journey. Here we will hear Maharishi Valmiki's Ramayana in a new way — not merely as an ancient legend, but woven with living questions that connect directly to our lives today, our decisions and our relationships.
The Ramayana is the creation of Maharishi Valmiki — the great saga of Prince Rama of Ayodhya, Sita, Lakshmana, Bharata and Hanuman, spread across seven kandas. In it there is kingdom and exile, love and separation, war and victory, and above all the question of dharma. This is not merely the story of kings, but the story of every human being who must make difficult choices in life.
Every episode is grounded in Manmatha Nath Dutt's celebrated prose translation, resting on the original Valmiki text preserved on Project Gutenberg. We have kept the story's original structure, order and completeness exactly intact, so you can trace every episode back to its authentic source. Where the language has been made simple and modern, the events and characters themselves have never been tampered with.
Each of our episodes is woven in two parts. In the first part I narrate the tale — its characters, events and turning points, with full vividness. In the second part we pause and reflect — why did the characters decide as they did, what dilemma stood before them, and how does that same dilemma appear today in our offices, families and society. Every reflection opens with a question and closes on a maxim you can carry into life.
One important note — the opening chapters of the Bala Kanda are this epic's 'executive summary.' Just as a vast document opens with a concise overview, Maharishi Valmiki compressed Rama's entire life arc into these early cantos. So the pace will feel swift and the episodes condensed. Rest assured — a few chapters on, the detailed scene-by-scene chronicle begins, and we have preserved that original compositional order exactly.
Have no worry about language. The tale will be told in simple, modern Hindi, and every line will carry its natural English rendering alongside — so that every member of the household, child or elder, can listen together. You need to know nothing in advance. The introduction of characters, the context of places and the meaning of difficult words — all of it will be opened up and explained within the story itself.
So this journey is not mere entertainment. Rama's exile teaches us what keeping a promise costs, Bharata's waiting teaches us that power is a trust held in keeping, and Hanuman's leap teaches us that true friendship makes the impossible possible. At the end of every chapter we will draw from these very episodes such maxims of leadership, patience, compassion and courage as you can try in your own life that very week.
And now, the hour of beginning is here. In the next chapter we open the first canto of the Bala Kanda — where Maharishi Valmiki, deep in austerity, asks the sage Narada who in this age is the complete man, filled with duty, truth and compassion. Narada's answer is — Rama. So settle in, hold your breath, and walk with me from that first question to that first tale.
Why pair every tale with a reflective question — why is the straight story not enough? In my view a story touches the mind, but a question transforms it. Hearing Rama's decision is memory; questioning it is self-examination. The dilemmas of promise versus compassion, of authority versus renunciation, repeat themselves today in our homes and workplaces, and the question becomes the bridge between those two worlds. This week, try this — after every episode, write one question in your diary and apply it to one decision of your own.
How should a listener hearing the Ramayana for the first time begin this journey? My advice is — listen with an open mind, unburdened by prior knowledge or fixed notions. Do not hold the characters at a distance as gods; bring them close as fellow humans — see your own dilemma in Rama's dilemma, recognize your own fear in Kaikeyi's fear. Hear the tale in order, for every chapter is the foundation of the next. While hearing the first episode, try this — choose one character whose situation mirrors your own, and keep an account of that character's decisions through the whole journey.
